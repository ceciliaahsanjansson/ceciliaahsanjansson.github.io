I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the University of Gothenburg. My research lies at the intersection of political economy, behavioral economics, and applied microeconomics. I study how people evaluate political information, form political beliefs and attitudes, and how these shape political and institutional behavior. My work combines survey and field experiments with large-scale administrative data.

Job Market Paper · working title

(Mis)information and Engagement with Public Institutions

Politically salient information shocks, such as misinformation, may shape how citizens engage with the state. This paper studies a large online campaign targeting Swedish social services and uses Swedish administrative data to examine whether families plausibly more exposed to the campaign changed their interactions with child-facing public institutions.
